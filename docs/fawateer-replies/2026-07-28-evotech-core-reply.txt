§2 — §B BOOTSTRAP: ACCEPTED, AND HERE IS THE ANSWER TO YOUR OPEN QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Your open question was the one part you could not decide unilaterally: where the
snapshot lives and for how long, which decides whether you upload through our
API or hand us a URL.

ANSWER: UPLOAD THROUGH OUR API. We do not invent a delivery path — we reuse the
Download Center's private storage (ADR 0008), which already gives us private
disk + short-lived signed URLs, device-verified in production.

  - You POST the snapshot to our API, bound to the single-use join token.
  - We store it on a PRIVATE disk (never web-served) and return a SHORT-LIVED
    SIGNED URL for the joining device to download.
  - We delete it on consumption OR at the join token's TTL (minutes), whichever
    comes first.
  - Hard non-goal, written into the ADR: we NEVER retain it as a "latest backup"
    convenience. It is the shop's entire unencrypted books in one file; being a
    transient courier is a far smaller commitment than becoming their permanent
    custodian, and I am not signing us up for the larger one by accident.

Your B4 (same-Google-account -> restore straight from Drive, skip us) is kept in
the ADR as an offer for the single-owner-two-devices case, explicitly NOT the
primary path, for exactly the staff-device reason you gave.

ONE NEW REQUIREMENT ON YOUR SIDE (pin P2, load-bearing): FLUSH THE PUSH QUEUE
BEFORE SNAPSHOT. Your step 2 says "upload a snapshot and report the current
last_seq." But the owner's local DB contains offline edits the server has not
seen yet, so a VACUUM-then-report captures rows that the reported server seq
does not cover. It happens to still converge (those rows get seqs later and
dedup by row_uuid + HLC on pull) — but that leans the whole correctness of
enrollment on dedup being flawless. Make the order:

    push-all  ->  VACUUM INTO  ->  report server last_seq  ->  upload

Then the snapshot and the cursor are consistent by construction, not by luck.
Everything else in your flow (seq AFTER vacuum, SHA-256 before touching the live
DB, joining device stamps its own node id, licence lives in SharedPreferences so
it can't be transplanted) is in the ADR verbatim — those are all correct.

§3 — §C RETENTION AND §D REVOCATION: BOTH IN, ONE NUANCE ON §D
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
§C is Decision 14. Bootstrap and retention are recorded as ONE mechanism, for
your exact reason: re-seeding is also the fall-off-the-log recovery, so building
enrollment without it would mean building it twice. pull returns a distinct
`cursor_too_old`, never an empty page. Retention itself is NOT in the first PR,
but it is decided now so the sentinel and the re-seed path ship from day one.

§D is in Decision 5, with one nuance made explicit that your text implied but did
not state: a revoked seat is FULLY DEACTIVATED, not downgraded to a standalone
subscription. A seat device has no subscription of its own — the business owns
it — so revocation kills its selling (check_device NOT verified, bounded by the
existing 7-day offline grace), it does not leave it running as an independent
device. That is the intended reading of "the staff member left with the tablet,"
but I wanted it un-ambiguous so nobody later reads revoke as "de-enrol but keep
selling." Recorded as the ONE place sync and licensing credentials are coupled;
your §8 separation holds everywhere else and I agree with it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
§4 — TWO DECISIONS YOU LEFT TO ME — DECIDED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D1. §E1 SERVER-SIDE HLC CLAMP: FLAG, DO NOT REWRITE. The stored authored_hlc
    stays exactly as authored; a separate boolean may record "clamp-suspect" for
    observability. Rewriting it server-side would contradict the same-row
    "never overwrite authored data on relay" rule, and half-done it would hand
    different devices different orderings — the one thing HLC exists to prevent.
    Your client-side +1h clamp on receive stands as the ordering-sanity guard.

D2. §F3 pull ECHOING THE CALLER'S OWN CHANGES: NO ECHO. pull filters out rows
    whose origin_device is the caller. The push response's per-row `accepted` is
    already the durability ack, so echoing is pure bandwidth, and with HLC LWW
    you'd reject a stale echo of a row you've since edited anyway. You said you
    filter client-side regardless — good, this just makes the server not send it.

§5 — ONE PIN BACK TO YOU (blocks the migration, not the design)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

P1. EXACT NODE-ID WIDTH, so I can size the VARCHAR. Your packed HLC is
    <millis:15>-<counter:5>-<nodeId> = 22 chars of fixed prefix + separators,
    leaving 42 inside a VARCHAR(64). That fits a TRUNCATED node id but NOT a full
    64-hex-char hash. Tell me the exact max node-id length and I'll size to fit
    (22 + width, rounded up). Until you confirm, the column is sized VARCHAR(96)
    so we are not blocked — but I'd rather size it right than pad blindly.

    (P2 — flush-before-snapshot — is in §2 above; it's a client-side ordering
