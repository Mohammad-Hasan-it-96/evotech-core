Short answer: YES. The owner's subscription covers the seats. A device that joins
an existing sync business does NOT need its own paid subscription. Your reading of
ADR 0011 is correct, and it is not just intent — it is the binding decision
(ADR 0011, Decision 2): "when business_id is set, the business row is the
authoritative source of subscription state ... check_device for an enrolled device
reports the business's expiry/verified."

You found a real gap: the column and the owner-side link shipped, but the two
halves that make a member actually covered were never built. They are now.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WE CHANGED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. enroll() now binds the joining device to the business's licence.
   - If the device already registered (it called create_device first and took its
     own trial), we LINK that row — set its business_id. No duplicate.
   - If the device never registered — the first-run "join a shop" case — we CREATE
     a minimal device_subscriptions row for it. Exactly as you asked: it carries
     NO name and NO phone (we do not invent them; the shop is already registered by
     the owner), and it gets NO trial of its own. The enrol request's device_id +
     the business's app_name are all the row needs.

2. check_device (and create_device) now read the BUSINESS for any linked device.
   Its expires_at / plan / is_verified come from the owner's business, not from the
   device's own row. So a member's missing or lapsed own-trial no longer locks it
   out. Same JSON shape as before — only the source changed.

3. Owner renewal reaches every seat.
   When the owner buys or renews, we carry the new expiry/plan onto the business,
   so all seats stay covered. A tier upgrade GROWS the allowance (1 -> 3 -> 5);
   a downgrade never shrinks it below what is enrolled, so no phone is stranded
   mid-shift. Without this, an owner could pay and still watch every phone lapse on
   the expiry that was seeded when they first turned on sync.

Revoking a seat still stops that phone at its next check_device (the coupling you
already knew about), bounded by your 7-day offline grace. That half was already
live; now the "covered" half matches it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
YOUR SPECIFIC ASKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- "CREATE the row when it is absent, not only link an existing one." Done — see #1.
  This is the part that unblocks a brand-new handset.
- "We have neither phone nor name and should not invent them." Agreed. The created
  row has both null. create_device's `phone: required` rule is NOT on this path —
  enrol does not go through create_device.
- Each phone holding its own paid subscription? No. The owner's covers them. The
  tier wording ("3 devices, one price") stands as sold.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS UNBLOCKS FOR YOU
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first-run screen you were waiting on. "Join a shop" can skip registration
entirely: the joining phone enrols, gets its seat, and check_device returns
verified from the owner's business — it never sees a plans screen. Build it against
"the owner's subscription covers the seats".
