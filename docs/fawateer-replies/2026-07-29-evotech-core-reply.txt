--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
§1 — CURSOR RULE: FIXED, AND THE TEST IS MOVED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Decision 13 step 2 now reads:

    push-all  ->  pull-to-current  ->  read OWNER'S OWN cursor C (before VACUUM)
              ->  VACUUM INTO  ->  upload(+C)

with the invariant stated as you framed it — the owner's cursor means "everything
<= C is applied to MY database," so everything <= C is in the snapshot by
construction and everything > C the joiner pulls, with no window where a row is
neither. The ADR now says explicitly that a server-side last_seq is wrong at
every point in the sequence because the server does not know which changes the
owner has APPLIED, and it carries your seq-101-in-neither example. "Read C before
the VACUUM" is in, with your reasoning for why after-is-unsafe / before-errs-only-
harmlessly.

The first-PR test is moved off the happy path. The ADR Consequences now pin it:
a THIRD device enrolling while a SECOND device has an unpulled change
outstanding — because the happy-path test passes under both the broken and the
fixed rule, which is exactly what made this dangerous.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
§2 — next_cursor = HIGHEST SEQ EXAMINED: PINNED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Decision 9 (pull) now states next_cursor is the highest seq EXAMINED, never the
highest RETURNED — a scan watermark — with your two reasons: (a) a page that is
all the caller's own rows filters to empty and a returned-only cursor never
advances, so the client loops on the same window forever and has_more cannot
rescue it; (b) it is what lets the owner's pull-to-current in §1 advance past its
own pushed rows, which under no-echo it never receives. Recorded as one fix with
§1, not two.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
§3 — P1 CLOSED: authored_hlc VARCHAR(40), origin_device VARCHAR(16)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Node id = first 16 hex of the SHA-256 device id. Decision 7 now sizes
authored_hlc VARCHAR(40) (15+1+5+1+16 = 38) and origin_device VARCHAR(16), with
your rationale (2.8x metadata on nine tables x every row, carried on the phone,
in every Drive backup, and now in every snapshot transiting us; ~10^-18
collision within a <=5-device business). The deterministic-prefix point is in:
no mapping table, the no-echo filter compares origin_device against
SUBSTR(device_id, 1, 16). And your fallback caveat is recorded AS an explicit
link between Decision 7 and Decision 5 — 'fallback_device_id' truncates to
'fallback_device_' identically for every fallback device, so reject-at-enrol is
what keeps the truncation scheme safe. The two decisions now reference each
other in the ADR so they cannot drift apart.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
§4 — H1 + H2: BOTH IN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
H1. Upload now requires the OWNER'S authenticated device token, with the join
    token as the binding rather than the credential. The ADR draws the split you
    flagged explicitly: enrol (joiner obtaining its sync token) stays
    join-token-authenticated; upload (an owner action) is a distinct
    owner-token-authenticated endpoint, and the two must not be conflated. Closes
    the photograph-the-QR / poisoned-snapshot race.

H2. Expected SHA-256 travels in the enrolment QR, computed by the owner locally
    and checked by the joiner against the server-delivered bytes — so integrity
    is genuinely owner->joiner and never trusts our stored hash, which only
    certifies transit. The ordering constraint is stated: the QR is rendered
    AFTER the upload, because the hash does not exist until the snapshot does.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
§5 — R1 + R2: BOTH IN THE ADR WORDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R1. The owner seat is not revocable — refused at the endpoint, not merely hidden
    in the UI — because revoking it orphans the business (live subscription, no
    device able to mint join tokens, no path back but operator intervention).

R2. Revocation does not repossess data, stated plainly: a revoked tablet keeps a
    complete local copy of the books; we stop future selling (check_device NOT
    verified, bounded by the 7-day offline grace) and stop it syncing, but
    nothing in this design can remote-wipe it. Recorded with your framing — the
    failure guarded against is non-technical: no one should later promise a
    shopkeeper a wipe capability the architecture does not have.
