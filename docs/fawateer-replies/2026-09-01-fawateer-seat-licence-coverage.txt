SUBJECT: a joined member device gets a seat but no subscription, so the
         licence gate still blocks it

Question first, because the answer decides a UI we are about to build:
**does a device that joins an existing sync business need its own paid
subscription, or is it covered by the owner's?**

ADR 0011 opens with "One subscription = one business owning N devices",
and the plan tiers sell 1/3/5 seats. Read plainly, that says the owner's
subscription covers the seats. The code does not do that yet.

WHAT THE CODE DOES TODAY

  onboardOwner()  -> creates the DeviceSeat AND links
                     device_subscriptions.business_id, "so check_device reads
                     the business thereafter" (your own docblock, §Decision 1/30).

  enroll()        -> creates the DeviceSeat and NOTHING ELSE. It never touches
                     device_subscriptions.

And `check_device` resolves purely by (device_id, app_name) against
device_subscriptions (DeviceSubscriptionService::find). A joined member device
has a seat but no subscription row tied to the business, so as far as licensing
is concerned it is an unknown device.

WHY IT LOOKS LIKE IT WORKS

A fresh phone calls create_device first and is given the free trial (Plan 006).
So a second phone joins, syncs, sells — and stops working when its own trial
ends, with a plans screen asking the shopkeeper to buy a second subscription
for a shop they have already paid for. In a field test the failure is weeks
away from the action that caused it.

WHAT WE THINK IS RIGHT

DECIDED ON OUR SIDE, 2026-09-01: the owner's subscription covers the seats.

`enroll()` should link the joining device the way `onboardOwner()` links the
owner: set `device_subscriptions.business_id`, and let `check_device` answer
from the business's subscription state (expiry, plan, verification) for any
device holding an active, non-revoked seat.

**Please CREATE the `device_subscriptions` row when it is absent**, rather than
only linking an existing one. This is the part that matters for a brand-new
handset, and it is not a detail: `create_device` validates `phone` as
`required`, so a phone that has never been registered cannot get a row without
asking its user for a name and a number. On a joining device we have neither
and should not invent them — the shop is already registered, by the owner. The
enrol request carries `device_id`, `app_name` and the proposed device name,
which is everything the row needs.

Without that, the first-run "join a shop" screen has to send the user through
the registration form first, which is exactly the detour it exists to remove.

Then:

  - the allowance is the only thing that limits seats, which is what the tiers
    are already sold on;
  - revoking a seat stops that phone selling at its next check, bounded by our
    existing 7-day offline grace — which is what `syncRevokeBody` already tells
    the owner happens, and today is not true for the licence half;
  - a member device that leaves the business falls back to its own row.

If instead each phone is meant to hold its own paid subscription, say so and we
will keep the current behaviour — but then the tier allowance and the wording
around it need rethinking on both sides, because "3 devices" currently reads to
a shopkeeper as "three phones, one price".

WHAT IS BLOCKED ON THE ANSWER

The first-run screen. We want a new phone to open on "create a new shop" or
"join a shop", instead of a registration form followed by a hunt through
Settings. If the owner's subscription covers seats, "join a shop" skips
registration entirely and the joining phone never sees a plans screen. If it
does not, joining has to register and take a trial first, and the screen is
only a reordering of what exists.

We are not building it until we know which.

RELATED, ALREADY HANDLED ON OUR SIDE (no action needed)

  - A linked phone no longer writes backups to Google Drive; the main phone
    owns the shop's backup history. Restore stays available on both.
  - A Drive restore on a linked phone now tells the owner to link the other
    phones again: restored rows carry `updated_at = ''` and so never replicate,
    and re-linking reseeds them through the bootstrap snapshot. We deliberately
    do NOT re-stamp and push a restored catalogue — that would claim this
    device authored every row at restore time and beat a genuine later edit on
    the other till under last-write-wins.
